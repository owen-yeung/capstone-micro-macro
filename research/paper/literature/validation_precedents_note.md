I found a useful set of methodological precedents; the strongest matches are not all in construction, but several closely mirror the logic of your revised design. The [paper collection](?artifact=Papers%20Using%20Validation%20Strategies%20Relevant%20to%20the%20Tendering%20Simulation) includes 12 papers with a concise description of each validation strategy and its transfer limits.

The closest conceptual fit is the operations/supply-chain study that explicitly separates human-equivalence testing from decision-process validation: its models could match some observed outcomes while exhibiting decision processes unlike participants’ processes. [^1] The strongest precedent for your controlled arms is the replication-to-prediction approach: it replays established human experiments, compares minimal architectures/components, and only then tests novel conditions. [^2] That maps especially well to your single-shot / same-role multi-step / multi-role contrast and your separation of settlement-linked feedback from no-feedback conditions.

Two other findings reinforce your scoring discipline. Across nine behavioral-operations experiments, LLMs could reproduce hypothesis-level effects while still diverging in the full human response distributions—so a plausible terminal decision or correct direction alone is an incomplete validity test. [^3] And the LLM simulation audit found that models often recognized the underlying experiment and that prompts could pre-determine outcomes, supporting your answer-key withholding, sealed ordering, and challenge-class controls. [^4] An independent real-world benchmark and comparison of grounded versus ungrounded agents offers a further precedent for your archival task-card grounding and explicit missing-information conditions. [^5]

One caveat: these papers support the *design logic*, not a claim that the tendering simulation has been behaviorally validated. In particular, several use human participant data, whereas your current expert-slice labels come from another model provider; that should remain a model-assisted credibility check, not be described as independent expert adjudication.
